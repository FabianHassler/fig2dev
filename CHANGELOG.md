# Changelog

## Unreleased

- PostScript and EPS: a JPEG picture draws in ghostscript. The driver wrote `W H sc` before the JPEG block, but the prolog never defined `sc` (genps's prolog has `/sc {scale} bind def`); it does now.
- TIFF: grey pictures of 2 and 4 bits (min-is-black and min-is-white) are read, as `tifftopnm` reads them (the `image` crate rejected them with "Bad TIFF format"). Samples are stretched to 0..255.
- JPEG over the pixel cap (2^25 pixels, 5793 x 5793 and more): PNG, other bitmap and PDF output now say `FILE: Bad JPEG format` and draw the placeholder, as a PNG over the cap does (before: no message, a flat grey box in bitmaps and a garbled picture in PDF). PostScript and EPS still embed the compressed data without decoding it, as genps does, so they draw such a file without a message.
- CMYK JPEG without an Adobe marker: bitmaps and the canvas take the samples as plain CMYK, as `readjpg.c`, PostScript and PDF do (the decoder of the `image` crate always took them as inverted, so such a file came out nearly black in PNG only). Files with the Adobe marker are unchanged (inverted in every output). The CMYK file is now decoded through `zune-jpeg`, the crate `image` already uses, as a direct dependency (no new crate in the tree).

## 0.9.7 — 2026-11-02

- TIFF pictures with an alpha channel keep their transparency like PNG ones (C's `tifftopnm | ppmtopcx` drops it): blended with what lies below in the canvas, PDF (soft mask) and bitmaps, composited over white in PostScript and EPS. Associated (premultiplied, ExtraSamples 1) alpha is un-premultiplied first. Palette TIFFs and TIFFs without alpha are unchanged.
- PNG with `tRNS`: the straight pixels are kept as for alpha PNGs, so a palette entry's own alpha value is used per pixel, and a grey or RGB picture with a transparent colour makes exactly the pixels of that stored colour transparent (before, the colour was compared after gamma correction and compositing, so other pixels of the same visible colour could turn transparent too). 16-bit PNGs with alpha were checked: they blend correctly.
- `fill_from_rgba` (the generic RGBA reader) keeps the straight alpha in `Pic::rgba` and no longer marks pixels of the background colour as the transparent colour (an opaque white pixel of a picture with alpha stayed "transparent").
- SVG: embedded PNG images are written with `raster::write_png` (RGB when nothing is transparent, best compression) instead of tiny-skia's always-RGBA default-compression encoder; alpha is kept where present.
- PNG pictures with an alpha channel (RGBA, grey + alpha) keep it in the canvas, PDF and bitmap output: partly transparent pixels are blended with what lies below (straight-alpha "over"), and the PDF image gets a soft mask. They came out too pale, since the reader composited the pixels over white (in libpng's linear light, as C does) and the scene then drew them opaque, and an opaque white pixel of such a picture was taken for the transparent colour. PostScript and EPS output still composite over white, as genps does (PostScript has no alpha). `Pic::rgba` holds the straight pixels; the other drivers' reading (emf, tk, ptk) is unchanged, and `-g` or `-N` still composite at read time.
- PNG export: written as RGB (colour type 2) when no pixel is transparent, RGBA otherwise, with the best compression and adaptive filtering (was always RGBA at the default compression): 2 to 7 times smaller on the sample figures (pines 30,230 to 8,028 bytes, wenig 205,447 to 62,210, greece 2,761,744 to 2,276,711). The pixels are unchanged; the output stays deterministic. New `raster::write_png`.

- Reader: a text keeps its leading blanks, as xfig's `f_read.c` reads them (the rest of the line after the separating blank; Fig 2.x to 3.2). C fig2dev's `"%d %n"` skips all blanks, so `4 ... 1200    abc\001` gave `abc` there and gives `   abc` here. Intended difference from fig2dev 3.2.9a: figures with such texts (indented text, a blank-only text) now differ from the C output by those blanks in the text drivers, PostScript, SVG and PDF. Fig 1.3 is unchanged (xfig's `%d %[^\n]` skips them too). The editing reader already did this.

## 0.9.6 — 2026-11-01

- `-E utf-16`: converts like iconv's `UTF-16` (a byte order mark decides, big endian without one; a lone last byte is dropped; the text ends at an unpaired surrogate) instead of failing on the paper size: the header's strings (version, paper size) are no longer decoded with `-E` (fig2dev converts texts only). A text with ASCII bytes only is never converted, whatever `-E` says (`conv_non_ascii`). Same as fig2dev 3.2.9a (libiconv) for BOM, big-endian default and surrogates, except: C also converts the text's terminating NUL, so every complete UTF-16 text got "The input string ... prematurely terminated." and an odd one an extra U+E400 character; neither is copied. glibc's iconv takes the host's byte order without a mark; this port always takes big endian. Not done: UTF-32/UCS-2/UCS-4, the "prematurely terminated" message for a real lone byte, and the EMF driver's EUC locale texts and fonts (`LANG=ja...`, `zh_CN`, `ko`; unrelated to `-E`).

- PPM, GIF, XPM and TIFF pictures: the colour map is `ppmtopcx`'s, as fig2dev reads it back from the PCX file. The colours of a picture with at most 256 come in netpbm's hash order (`ppm_hashpixel`: bucket `(r*1089 + g*33 + b) mod 20023`, a bucket's colours newest first, taken at the PPM's own maxval before scaling), not in order of first use; the map is padded with black to 2, 4, 8 or 16 entries (the PCX bit depth), else 256, and `numcols` counts the padding (it was the number of colours). `-N` greys every entry of the map afterwards, as `_read_pcx` does (it greyed the pixels before indexing, so equal greys merged). Checked against fig2dev 3.2.9a's PostScript (the `/Indexed` colour table) and against `ppmtopcx` 10.86 on random pictures of 1 to 256 colours, PPM, PGM and PBM, maxval 15 to 65535: same order. TIFF with a palette (`pnmtotiff`'s default; the `image` crate reads none) is read by fig2dev itself: one sample of 1, 2, 4 or 8 bits in strips, uncompressed, LZW, PackBits or Deflate, the 16-bit colour map scaled as `tifftopnm` does (direct dependencies `tiff` and `weezl`, both already used through `image`); the pictures rasterise pixel for pixel as fig2dev 3.2.9a's with `tifftopnm`.

- `-j` (PostScript and EPS): Japanese and Korean text in composite fonts, as `genps.c`. In an EUC locale (`LC_ALL`, `LC_CTYPE` or `LANG` = `ja_JP.eucJP`, `ja_JP.EUC`, `ja_JP`, `ja_JP.ujis`, `ja`, `ko_KR.eucKR`, `ko_KR.EUC`, `ko_KR`, `ko`) the locale's prologue (`japanese.ps` or `korean.ps`, built in; no `FIG2DEV_LIBDIR` needed) is added when a text has non-ASCII characters, and Times-Roman, Times-Bold and default-font texts are set in `CompositeRoman`/`CompositeBold` with `\377\001`/`\377\000` switching to the EUC font. Without an input encoding the bytes of the file are used (as C); a Unicode text (`#encoding: UTF-8`, `-E`) is converted to the locale's charset. Other locales: "Unable to open postscript prologue file LOCALE.ps" and plain text. Same as C except: bytes beyond ASCII are octal escapes (same PostScript string, not raw bytes); no spurious "Error reading file ... The output might be broken." after a successful copy (C tests `!fclose()`); the message names the file (C's lacks the argument); LaTeX font 1 (roman) uses `CompositeRoman` (C: bold). Not done: PDF output (written natively, with no CJK fonts to switch to) accepts `-j` and ignores it.

## 0.9.5 — 2026-10-31

- New language `-L asy` (B16): the figure as an Asymptote program (`asy -f pdf figure.asy`; fig2dev has no such driver, so this one is original). Coordinates are Fig units (y turned upwards) at `unitsize(S bp)`, so the magnification (`-m`) is the unit size; line widths, dashes and font sizes are in bp. Supported: polylines, polygons, boxes, rounded boxes, arcs (open and pie wedge), circles, ellipses (also turned), X-splines (as the polylines the reader makes; older spline types converted the same way), arrowheads of every type as paths (calc_arrow, with the shaft clipped even-odd so it does not poke through), dash and dot styles, caps and joins, standard colours as `rgb("rrggbb")` and user colours as pens `xfigcN`, shades and tints mixed as in the other drivers, fill patterns 41-62 as tilings (`patterns.asy`, one per pattern and pen colour, from the PostScript driver's tiles), depth order, texts (PostScript fonts as `font("T1","ptm","m","n")` with the TeX drivers' `\usefont` arguments, LaTeX fonts as `fontcommand`, special texts unquoted, other texts with LaTeX's special characters quoted, set on their baseline and rotated about it), pictures as `graphic(…)` labels (`settings.tex="pdflatex"` when a bitmap is used), and the options `-b` (border), `-F`, `-f` (the font by name; the C TikZ driver stores the name where the `\usefont` arguments belong), `-i`, `-w`, `-O`, `-o`, `-T`, `-G`. Paths of more than 200 points are built from an array because Asymptote's parser overflows its stack on a chain of about 1,000 `--`. Not done: pattern phase relative to the object (tilings start at the page corner, as in fig2dev's PostScript), `-C`, `-P`.

## 0.9.4 — 2026-10-31

- PDF: centred and right-justified text in the standard 14 fonts (Times, Helvetica, Courier; the PDF names them and does not embed them) is placed with the Adobe Core 14 AFM advance widths, which is how the viewer draws them, instead of the widths of the installed or fallback font (`scene/afm.rs`). Left-justified text is unchanged.
- PDF pictures in PostScript and EPS output: axial and radial shadings (as the fill of a path, a shading pattern, or the `sh` operator) are written as PostScript Level 3 `shfill` vectors (the EPS says `%%LanguageLevel: 3`) instead of turning the whole page into one image. Transparency, function-based and mesh shadings, shadings used as strokes, and tiling patterns still make the page an image. PDF pictures encrypted with an empty user password (RC4, AES) are read, as hayro does; tests added.
- Hardening (B11), checked against fig2dev 3.2.9a built from source: `-L box` writes the whole-figure comments as `print_comments("% ", ...)` does (`%  text ` with the comment's bytes as read; was `% text`): 0 differences on the 2,554 sample figures (1,041 before). Pictures compressed as `.zip`, `.bz2`, `.bz` or `.xz` are read through `unzip -p`, `bunzip2 -c` and `unxz -c` as `open_stream` does (run directly, no shell; not on wasm). `-E ascii` (and `us-ascii`, `ANSI_X3.4-1968`, ...) converts strictly as iconv: text stops at the first byte beyond 0x7f with "Error converting string ...: Illegal byte sequence"; that message and the "Unable to convert" one show the text's bytes as they are (also non-UTF-8 bytes); a charset name with blanks is refused as iconv does. Left: netpbm's palette order for PPM, TIFF, XPM and GIF pictures (first use here), `-E utf-16` and the multibyte charsets' error positions.
- Fill patterns (41-62) in smoothed bitmaps (`-S 2`, `-S 4`): the pattern lines are drawn hard-edged at the bitmap's own resolution and enlarged, as ghostscript draws them (it ignores `GraphicsAlphaBits` inside a pattern cell), so inside a shape the pixels equal the unsmoothed bitmap's; only the shape's edge is smoothed. On a 22-pattern sheet against fig2dev (C, with gs) the share of pixels differing by more than a third of the range falls from 29.5% to 26.6% (`-S 2`) and from 26.1% to 25.2% (`-S 4`), unsmoothed 23.6%. New public field `RasterOptions::pattern_k` (1 for no supersampling).
- Bugs of fig2dev 3.2.9a no longer copied (the port follows what C means, not what it got wrong):
  - SVG: a line with arrows was clipped by the previous object's other arrowhead too (C keeps the arrowheads in function statics); each object now clips only its own.
  - LaTeX, epic, pict2e, PiCTeX, TikZ: a Symbol or ZapfDingbats character from a UTF-8 file is one `\charNNN` (`±` → `\char177`; C wrote one per UTF-8 byte, `\char194\char177`), as the SVG driver already did; a Latin-1 file whose text needs converting keeps its codes (C took it for UTF-8 and dropped them). Turning UTF-8 into Latin-1 drops only a character Latin-1 lacks, not the characters after it.
  - MetaPost: the forward arrowhead of an approximated spline sits on the last point (C put it on the last point but one).
  - Gerber: `%SF…*%` and circular apertures `%ADD…C,…*%` end in `*%` (C wrote `%` and `%*`); the `D02` that ends a filled polygon or an arc ends its block (C left it on the next block's line), and no empty block is written before a flash.
  - `-h -L mf`: `-Y ymax` (C's help says `-Y xmax`).
  - SVG: shades and tints of a colour with red in it (fill 1-19, 21-39) had a wrong colour (C's blue mask let the red byte in: a half shade of red was `#7f807f`, now `#7f0000`).
  - Tk: `-g` puts `-bg` inside `[canvas …]` (C wrote it after the `]`, a Tcl error) with 8-bit channels (C divided the 16-bit ones by 255: white was `#101101101`).
  - PSTricks: user colours are divided by 255 (C: 256, so 255 never reached 1).
  - DXF: rounded boxes span their corners (C compared the flipped y with the raw y); ellipses take the longer radius as the major axis (ratio at most 1, as DXF requires) and are turned by their angle (C ignored it); the picture warning says DXF (C: "IBMGL").
  - LaTeX: dark brown is `.5,.19,0` (128, 48, 0; C: `.17`).
  - PiCTeX, TextYL, epic (short arcs): arc arrowheads point along the arc as in the SVG driver (C turned them over by mixing y-up and y-down conventions); epic rounds both coordinates of the arrowhead's base.
  - TikZ: open arcs keep their cap style (C tested the arc type the wrong way round, so wedges got it instead); turned ellipses keep a tenth of a degree (C rounded to whole degrees).
  - epic, PiCTeX: turned ellipses are turned (a quarter turn swaps the radii, other angles draw a 72-point polygon; C drew every ellipse unturned).
  - EMF: a turned text's bounds are its four corners (C took the end's y with the wrong sign); arc direction and the arrowhead test are computed in 64 bits (C's int overflowed beyond about 38 inches and turned arcs round).
  - CGM: the standard colours are xfig's 8-bit values (C truncated its float table: 175 for 176, 214 for 215).
  - MetaFont: shaded polylines write `unfilled polyline` (C: `unfilledpolyline`, not mfpic); ellipses are turned by their angle (C wrote 0).
  - ge: splines are written in the same units as everything else (C wrote Fig units, about 4.7 times too large).
  - PiCTeX: a black-filled box keeps its border width after the fill (C set the Fig thickness as points).
  - EPS pictures: the size covers the whole points the bounding box touches (C truncated a fractional box a point short), as for PDF pictures.
